Online courses have a completion problem. Depending on the study, somewhere between 5% and 15% of people who start a self-paced course reach the final lesson. That isn't a character flaw. It's a design problem, and design problems have design solutions.

Here are six habits I recommend to every learner I coach. None of them require more motivation. They just make the next lesson easier to start than to skip.

## 1. Decide when, not just what

"I'll do a lesson this week" is a wish. "I'll do a lesson on Tuesday at 7:30 a.m. with my coffee" is a plan. Research on _implementation intentions_ shows that pairing a goal with a specific time and place roughly doubles follow-through.

Put two or three short sessions on your calendar and treat them like meetings with your future self.

## 2. Keep sessions short

Twenty focused minutes beats a two-hour Saturday marathon. Short sessions are easier to start, easier to protect and, thanks to the _spacing effect_, better for long-term memory.

<Callout title="Learnora tip">
  Most Learnora lessons are under 15 minutes on purpose. One lesson plus a few notes is a complete session.
</Callout>

## 3. Take notes in your own words

Copying the slide is not note-taking. After each lesson, write two or three sentences explaining the idea as if to a colleague. If you can't, rewatch the part that lost you. This is called _elaborative encoding_, and it's one of the most reliable ways to make ideas stick.

## 4. Test yourself early

Quizzes feel like evaluation, but they're actually one of the best learning tools we have. Retrieving an answer strengthens memory far more than rereading. Take the quiz before you feel ready, then review only what you missed.

## 5. Build something small

Every course should end with something you made: a dashboard, a prototype, a short presentation. Projects turn passive knowledge into skills, and they give you something to show.

## 6. Make your progress visible

Progress bars aren't just decoration. Seeing how far you've come is a quiet but powerful motivator, especially in the messy middle of a course.

> The goal isn't to study more. It's to make starting the next lesson the easiest thing you'll do today.

---

Start small this week: pick one course, book two 20-minute sessions, and finish one lesson in each. That's how every finished course begins.
